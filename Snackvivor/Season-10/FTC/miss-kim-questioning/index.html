This question is for everyone...Beyond alliances, challenges, and tribals, what is the single, most unexpected thing you,ve learned about your own personal strength, weakness, and character while playing this game? How will that lesson change the way you approach your life once leaving this game?
Dorm 10/25/2025 2:19 PM
Thank you for the question Miss Kim. I’ve been slow to answer it because frankly, I’ve been thinking really really hard about it since it was posed. ORGs are a mixed bag for me. I love meeting people, love competing, but it genuinely stresses me out beyond measure. I’ve gotten legitimately sick this season due to the stress of it all, but despite that I wouldn’t trade it away. I always have faith in myself, and do the best I can, but I’ve started to learn how to use my anxiety as fuel rather than fear it. I spent so long trying to ignore anxiousness, or not think about it. There were times I’d take a break from the game, push it all down, but then think about it and it would all explode out again in these anxious spikes. I learned I can play a game I’m proud of, a strong game, a meaningful game and make friends with so many wonderful people at the same time. I learned I can play my absolute heart out and give it my absolute all just for my own sake. Most importantly, I’m learning how to believe in myself more. To acknowledge the anxiety, register it’s there, but move forward regardless. I have a tendency to double check myself constantly even when I know I’m right because I can’t trust myself. It’s lost me challenges in orgs before. I seek reassurance and validation and comfort in ensuring I’m doing things to perfection. But this season wasn’t perfect, no ORG can be. It was flawed, it was messy, and I’m still proud of it. I believed in myself, and just kept moving forward. I have a tendency in life to have a lot of big plans, get overwhelmed by the scope, then never get started. You can’t really do that in ORGs, you need to keep moving forward. There wasn’t time to beat myself up, or be harsh to myself like I have in the past. I just had to keep going, fight as hard as I could, and I created something I can be proud of. I don’t want to lose that after this game, I want to be able to keep that strength. I’ve always been someone who lives for others more than myself, it’s partially why I want to be a teacher. I want to be able to help people like me who needed help. But I’m also beginning to trust myself more, and realize that it’s ok to do things for myself too. It’s ok to believe in myself and have faith in my decisions. In a game about trust and betrayal, the person I learned to trust most was myself, which was a huge step for me. It’s not something I want to lose going forward. You and Sean have a knack for getting me in my feels and rambling lmao. I nearly wrote at the end there “if I didn’t answer right, let me know, and I’ll add more” on complete reflex. But I don’t need to seek reassurance, and I just need to keep believing in myself. So that’s my answer. Thank you for the question.
